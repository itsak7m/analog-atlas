Analog Atlas · Field method

From satellite screening to ground truth.

Concept · the rover is not built yet

Satellites see a whole planet, but only from above. A rover sees one place, but up close. Our method uses both: the atlas finds Earth places that match a Moon or Mars target, and a low-cost rover goes there, measures the ground itself and calibrates the satellite data.

Why a rover

What orbit cannot see

  • Pixels of 10 m to 1 km mix rock, sand and shadow.
  • Dust and air blur the surface signal.
  • No view under the soil, or inside lava caves.

What the rover adds

  • Rock spectrum from 5 cm away.
  • Soil moisture, salt and pH under the surface.
  • Slope and roughness of the last metres, where a lander or rover actually moves.

The rover

A 60 cm, six-wheel rover with a rocker-bogie suspension, the design that has driven on Mars since Sojourner (1997), then Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity and Perseverance. We keep the proven design and change the job: instead of exploring another planet, it measures Earth analog sites cheaply, with off-the-shelf parts.

How the data travels

Sensors→ESP32 packet (value, unit, position, time)→SD card log + upload to the server→Analog Atlas score

Every value carries its unit, source and type: measured on site, derived from a map, or not available. The same rule as the app: nothing is invented.

How we use AI

Now: calibration

Pairs of field readings and the satellite pixel over the same spot teach a model how much the satellite value differs from the ground. The corrected values then improve the score for every site of the same type.

Later: reverse inference

With enough pairs, the model can work the other way: from orbital data alone, it ranks Moon and Mars areas that no rover has visited, with an uncertainty for each, to suggest where missions look first. A ranking, not a fact.

Working with satellites and CubeSats

The rover does not replace orbiters or CubeSats. Satellites give the wide map; the rover is a ground-truth point that checks it. One field day can calibrate a whole scene of a satellite image over a similar terrain.

Sensor alternatives
  • Low-cost spectrometer AS7265x → a lab or field spectroradiometer for higher spectral resolution.
  • TFmini single-point LiDAR → a scanning LiDAR or stereo camera for a full 3D map faster.
  • Hobby gas sensors → calibrated gas analysers if gases become part of the score.
Limits
  • Gravity, radiation and vacuum cannot be copied on Earth; every analog breaks there.
  • The map resolution of the LiDAR and IMU still has to be validated in the field.
  • No rover data exists yet. The first field test is planned at Harrat al-Shaam and near Wadi Rum, Jordan.
